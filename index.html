Rhythmic Gymnastics

What is Rhythmic Gymnastics?

Rhythmic gymnastics is a mainly female Olympic sport that combines ballet, dance, and acrobatics using five different apparatuses.

Apparatuses

  • Rope
  • Ball
  • Hoop
  • Clubs
  • Ribbon

Individual and Groups

You can be an individual gymnast performing separate routines with different apparatuses.

Or you can be part of a group of five gymnasts performing two routines together.
In group routines, you can also have 2-5 gymnasts, but only if you are not in the International/National Elite Stream.
You must either use the same apparatus for everyone or have two gymnasts using one type of apparatus and three using another. However, you cannot use rope unless you are a Junior or Pre-Junior.
Also, if you are in the Regional/Provincial Development Program, everyone in your group must use the same apparatus, including rope.

Levels

There are different levels you can compete in, depending on your age and whether you are in the International/National Elite Stream or the Regional/Provincial Development Program.

For the International/National Elite Stream, there are four categories: Novice/Aspire (ages 10-11), Pre-Junior (ages 12-13), Junior FIG (ages 14-15), and Senior FIG/Olympic Level (ages 16+).

For the Regional/Provincial Development Program, there are Levels 1-2 (Pre-Competitive), Levels 3-4 (Beginner Competitive), Levels 5-6 (Intermediate Competitive), and Levels 7-8 (Advanced Development).

In the Regional/Provincial Development Program, there are also levels such as 3A, 3B, and 3C. These levels are divided into different categories based on the gymnast's age and skill level. This helps gymnasts compete against others who are at a similar stage in their training.

The evolution of Rhythmic Gymnastics

  1. In the 1940s, rhythmic gymnastics was developed as a competitive activity in the Soviet Union (originally known as modern or artistic gymnastics).

  2. In 1961, rhythmic gymnastics was formally recognized by the International Gymnastics Federation (FIG).

  3. In 1963, the first World Rhythmic Gymnastics Championships were held in Budapest, Hungary.
  4. In 1984, rhythmic gymnastics became an official Olympic sport at the Los Angeles Games, but only individual routines were included.

  5. In 1996, the group competition was added as an Olympic event at the Atlanta Games.

Fun Facts

  • Rope is completely banned from the International/National Elite Stream, except for Juniors and Pre-Juniors in group routines.

  • Historically, Eastern European countries, such as Bulgaria, Ukraine, and Russia, have dominated the sport. However, countries like Germany, Italy, Israel, Brazil, and Japan have become strong competitors on the modern international stage.

  • Men's rhythmic gymnastics, particularly the Japanese style, has active national and regional competitions. The sport is also growing internationally in countries like Japan, Spain, Russia, and the United States, although it is not yet officially part of the Olympic program.
