Rhythmic Gymnastics

What is Rhythmic Gymnastics?

Rhythmic gymnastics is a mainly female Olympic sport that is a mix of Ballet, dance, and acrobatics with 5 apparatuses.

Apparatuses

  • Rope
  • Ball
  • Hoop
  • Clubs
  • Ribbon

Individual and Groups

You can be an individual gymnast performing separate routines with multiple apparatuses.

Or you can be in a group of five performing two routines together.

Levels

There are different levels you can be in but it depends on your age and whether you are in the International/National Elite Stream or the Regional/Provincial Development Program.

For the International/National Elite Stream, there are four categories: Novice/ Aspire the age (Ages 10-11), Pre-Junior (Ages 12-13), Junior FIG (Ages 14-15), and Senior FIG/Olympic Level (Ages 16+).

For the Regional/Provincial Development program, there are Levels 1-2 (Pre-Competitive), levels 3-4 (Beginner Competitive), levels 5-6 (Intermediate Competitive), levels 7-8 (Advanced Development).

Fun facts

  • Rhythmic gymnastics was reconized as a sport in 1963.
  • In 1984, rhythmic gymnastics became an Olympic sport and it was only Individual.
  • The group competition was added to the Olympics in 1996.
  • Historically, Eastern European nations—such as Bulgaria, Ukraine, and Russia—have dominated the sport, though countries like Germany, Italy, Israel, Brazil, and Japan have emerged as powerhouse contenders on the modern international stage.
